## Section 3 — Purpose

**H2 ·** Fun on the day. Still useful on Monday.

Games for the sake of games can be fun on the day and forgotten by Monday. When every activity ties back to a real objective, such as helping a new team trust each other or getting departments talking, the laughter becomes a shared experience your people keep referring back to.

Facilitation is more than running games. It is reading the room and guiding a group toward an outcome, so everyone takes part, whatever their role or language.

- ✓ We read the room and adjust in real time
- ✓ Everyone takes part, whatever their role
- ✓ Facilitated in English and Mandarin
- ✓ Every segment is debriefed back to work

## Section 5 — Collaboration

**H2 ·** Stronger collaboration across teams

We design challenges that only work when people pool what they know, across teams and seniority. Nobody can win them alone, so the group has to talk, plan and trust each other.

The debrief ties what happened in the game to how your teams work together on Monday, so the day changes habits as well as moods.

- ✓ Challenges that need every team member
- ✓ Mixed teams across departments
- ✓ A debrief linked to real work
- ✓ Facilitated from start to finish

## Section 6 — Communication

**H2 ·** Clearer communication, learned through play

Activities that reward listening, clear instructions and checking in, so people notice their own habits while they play. We keep it light, and the lessons stick because everyone has just lived them.

Good for teams that work across functions, sites or time zones, where small misunderstandings slow everything down.

- ✓ Listening and instruction games
- ✓ Habits made visible, not lectured
- ✓ Light, fun and practical
- ✓ Takeaways the team agrees on

## Section 7 — Onboarding

**H2 ·** Onboarding new hires and merged teams

For new hires and merged teams, we mix people on purpose and give them reasons to talk. Within an hour, names have faces and the newest colleague has a story to tell.

We can run it as part of an induction day, a merger kick-off or a regional get-together.

- ✓ Deliberately mixed groups
- ✓ Icebreakers that do not feel forced
- ✓ Works for inductions and mergers
- ✓ Bilingual where teams need it

## Section 18 — FAQ

**H2 ·** Questions teams ask

**H3 ·** How much does corporate team building cost in Singapore?

From S$88 per participant for a 2–3 hour facilitated session, usually up to S$128. Every proposal is quoted for your group, objectives and venue.

**H3 ·** How is your team building different from a standard activity package?

We start with your objectives, not an activity list. After a short conversation about your people, group size, venue and budget, we design the session around the outcome you want.

**H3 ·** What group sizes do you work with?

Programmes are planned around your headcount, from a single department to a company-wide event. Share your expected numbers in the enquiry form and we will recommend a format that keeps everyone involved.

**H3 ·** Can sessions be run in English and Mandarin?

Yes. Our founder, De Zhong, facilitates and hosts in both English and Mandarin, which helps mixed-language teams take part comfortably.

**H3 ·** Where do sessions take place?

At your office, a hotel or event venue, or another location that suits your programme. If you still need a venue, we can recommend options through our partner network.

**H3 ·** How long is a session?

Most facilitated sessions run two to three hours; offsite programmes can run longer.

**H3 ·** Can team building be part of our D&D?

Yes. Team bonding games can be woven into your dinner and dance, offsite or anniversary, led by a professional host.

**H3 ·** How far in advance should we enquire?

As early as you can, especially for year-end and festive periods when dates fill up quickly. Share your preferred dates in the enquiry form and we will confirm availability.

**H3 ·** Will we receive a proposal and quotation?

Yes. Once we understand your objectives and requirements, we prepare a tailored proposal with a recommended programme and a quotation.
