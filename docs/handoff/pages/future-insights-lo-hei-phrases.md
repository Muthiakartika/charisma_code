**H1 ·** Lo hei phrases: what to say as you toss the yu sheng

**By Chia De Zhong** · <mark>January 2027</mark>

Every Chinese New Year, tables across Singapore stand up, chopsticks raised, and toss the yu sheng as high as they can. The higher the toss, the better the year ahead. What turns the toss into a celebration is what everyone calls out while doing it. Here are the lo hei phrases I lead with from the stage, what they mean, and how to lead the toss at your own table.

**H2 ·** What is lo hei?

Lo hei (捞起) is Cantonese for "tossing up". Yu sheng is a raw fish salad served at Chinese New Year dinners in Singapore and Malaysia. The ingredients are added one by one, each with a wish for the year, and then everyone tosses the salad together. Company dinners, family reunions and friends' gatherings all do it, usually at the start of the meal.

**H2 ·** Six lo hei phrases and what they mean

**H3 ·** 年年有余 · nián nián yǒu yú

Abundance year after year. Usually said as the raw fish goes on: 鱼 (fish) sounds like 余 (surplus).

**H3 ·** 大吉大利 · dà jí dà lì

Great luck and great profit. A favourite for business tables. <mark>Said as the pomelo goes on.</mark>

**H3 ·** 步步高升 · bù bù gāo shēng

Rising higher step by step. A wish for promotions and a better year at work.

**H3 ·** 风生水起 · fēng shēng shuǐ qǐ

Good fortune that keeps growing.

**H3 ·** 招财进宝 · zhāo cái jìn bǎo

Wealth and treasure coming in. <mark>Said as the pepper and cinnamon are sprinkled.</mark>

**H3 ·** 万事如意 · wàn shì rú yì

May everything go as you wish. A good closing wish before the final toss.

**H2 ·** How to lead the toss at your table

- Wait until everyone is standing with chopsticks ready.
- Add the ingredients one at a time and say each phrase clearly, so the table can repeat it.
- Say the Mandarin phrase first, then the meaning in English, so everyone at a mixed table knows what they are wishing for.
- On the final toss, count down and get everyone shouting "lo hei!" together. Mess on the table is part of the luck.

**H2 ·** Want someone to lead it for you?

At company Chinese New Year dinners I lead the lo hei from the stage in English and Mandarin, so every table knows what to say and when to toss. It is one of the moments people remember from the night.
